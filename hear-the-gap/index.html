Hear the Gap

When someone's hearing fades, the TV creeps up and they don't notice. This measures how far it has crept, then plays that same jump back to them so they can feel what the rest of the house lives with.

Step 1

Find the gap

The gap is how much louder their TV setting is than the setting everyone else would pick, in decibels (dB). Pick whichever way is easiest.

Type the number on the TV's volume display for each person.

TV volume numbers are not a standard unit. Most sets change by roughly 0.5 to 1.5 dB per step, so 1 is a fair guess. The other two methods measure the real thing.

Two people set the same sound to the level they would choose for the TV. Use this device's speaker, sit where you would sit, and leave the device's own volume buttons alone between the two turns.

Step 3

Quick hearing check

Five beeping tones, from low to high, each starting silent and getting louder. Press the button the moment you hear the beeps. It takes about a minute.

A phone can't measure hearing on its own, so someone with good hearing does it first as the yardstick. Same device, same volume setting (about half), same quiet room.

What to do with this

  • Book a proper hearing test. Many opticians and pharmacies do them free, and a GP can refer you to audiology.
  • Hearing loss usually takes the high pitches first. That removes the consonants, so speech sounds mumbled and turning it up only makes the mumble louder. Hearing aids put back the pitches that are missing.
  • Until then, turn on subtitles, or try a TV speech mode or a small speaker beside their chair.

This is a demonstration and a rough screen. It is not a medical test and can't diagnose anything.
